Fridgeboard concepts

One shared list for an Android phone and an iPhone, replacing the whiteboard on the fridge. It's a free web app you install to your home screen, with Google sign-in and Firebase sync. Every concept includes the two lists (Grocery and Nice-to-have), the stores, live sync, and an activity log. They differ in which of those the screen is built around.

Concept A

Fridge Board

The whiteboard, in your pocket. Items appear in a handwritten style on a lined board, in the marker color of whoever added them. Store "magnets" sit next to each item. Draw a line through an item to check it off. Nice-to-have items are pinned in a corner of the same board.

Strengths
  • No learning curve. It's your current habit
  • Who added what is obvious at a glance
  • Has the most personality of the four
Trade-offs
  • Gets messy past about 25 items
  • Hard to fit prices and aisles into the look
  • Handwritten text is harder to read in a hurry

Concept B

Aisle Flow

Built for use in the store. Pick a store and the list re-sorts by aisle, with large check targets, checked items sliding down, and a running estimated total. When either of you taps "Start trip," the other gets a notification: "Alex is at Walmart." Nice-to-have is a tab, and its items show up here when they're tagged for the store you're in.

Strengths
  • Fastest trips, one-handed
  • Store filter fits your 4-store habit
  • Matches the industry standard (AnyList, Bring!)
Trade-offs
  • Plainer look than A or D
  • Aisle numbers need per-store data or learning
  • Activity history is a level deeper

Concept C

Price Board

Built around saving money. Each row is an item, each column is a store, and the cheapest price is highlighted. The app offers a split ("buy these 3 at Walmart, these 2 at Harris Teeter") and a one-tap cart handoff where the store supports it. Every price shows its source and age, because online and in-store prices differ.

Strengths
  • Shows the value of pricing right away
  • Good fit with a 4-store routine
  • Builds toward budgets and price history
Trade-offs
  • Depends on API access (Target is hard)
  • Each item must be matched to a product
  • Poor main screen. Better as a sheet

Concept D

Together Feed

Built around the two of you. The home screen is the activity log: "Sam added 4 items," with each item shown below, plus quick reactions. Nice-to-have becomes a swipe deck. Swipe right to move an item to Grocery, left to keep it for later. Good for deciding together without texting.

Strengths
  • Puts your logging requirement front and center
  • Feels personal, made for two
  • Nice-to-have actually gets reviewed
Trade-offs
  • The list itself is one tap away
  • Feed gets noisy without grouping
  • Weakest in the store
Recommendation

Three tabs, built for how you shop

The concepts aren't mutually exclusive. Since you walk Harris Teeter in store and use pickup at Target, the list screen changes with the store. Prices now come mostly from receipts, so the price grid from C becomes a Spending tab. Per-person colors from A appear on every screen.

List: Aisle Flow (B)Harris Teeter: sorted by aisle, shared live trip or solo trip. Target: pickup checklist with Open in Target buttons.
Us: Together Feed (D)Grouped activity log with reactions. The Nice-to-have swipe deck lives here.
Spending: newSnap a receipt, review the parsed lines, and see monthly totals, budget, and price history by store.

New screen

Receipts and spending

Take a photo of a paper receipt, or a screenshot of a Target pickup or Amazon order. Gemini reads it into lines, the app checks that the lines add up to the total, and matches each line to an item on your list. Matched items are checked off and their "last paid" price updates. That's how Target gets real prices without an API.

You get
  • Monthly total against your budget
  • Spend by store, category, and person
  • Price history per item across stores
  • VIC and Circle savings totaled
Watch for
  • Card digits are cropped off before upload
  • Gemini's free tier may use data to improve Google's products
  • Long receipts need 2–3 photos

Questions for the two of you

Answers to these will shape the design

  1. Logging: should every change be logged, with multi-item adds grouped into one entry?
  2. Do you still shop at Walmart? If not, it comes out of the first version.
  3. Should checked-off items stay crossed out until the trip ends, or disappear right away?
  4. Do you want a monthly grocery budget to track against? How much?
  5. Receipt reading: Gemini's free tier (most accurate, card info cropped out first), or fully on-device (private, less accurate)?
  6. Which iOS version is on her iPhone? Push notifications need 16.4 or later.
  7. Keep the name Fridgeboard?
