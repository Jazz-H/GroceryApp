Concept A

Fridge Board

The whiteboard, in your pocket. Items appear in a handwritten style on a lined board, in the marker color of whoever added them. Store "magnets" sit next to each item. Draw a line through an item to check it off. Nice-to-have items are pinned in a corner of the same board.

Strengths
  • No learning curve. It's your current habit
  • Who added what is obvious at a glance
  • Has the most personality of the four
Trade-offs
  • Gets messy past about 25 items
  • Hard to fit prices and aisles into the look
  • Handwritten text is harder to read in a hurry

Concept B

Aisle Flow

Built for use in the store. Pick a store and the list re-sorts by aisle, with large check targets, checked items sliding down, and a running estimated total. When either of you taps "Start trip," the other gets a notification: "Alex is at Walmart." Nice-to-have is a tab, and its items show up here when they're tagged for the store you're in.

Strengths
  • Fastest trips, one-handed
  • Store filter fits your 4-store habit
  • Matches the industry standard (AnyList, Bring!)
Trade-offs
  • Plainer look than A or D
  • Aisle numbers need per-store data or learning
  • Activity history is a level deeper

Concept C

Price Board

Built around saving money. Each row is an item, each column is a store, and the cheapest price is highlighted. The app offers a split ("buy these 3 at Walmart, these 2 at Harris Teeter") and a one-tap cart handoff where the store supports it. Every price shows its source and age, because online and in-store prices differ.

Strengths
  • Shows the value of pricing right away
  • Good fit with a 4-store routine
  • Builds toward budgets and price history
Trade-offs
  • Depends on API access (Target is hard)
  • Each item must be matched to a product
  • Poor main screen. Better as a sheet

Concept D

Together Feed

Built around the two of you. The home screen is the activity log: "Sam added 4 items," with each item shown below, plus quick reactions. Nice-to-have becomes a swipe deck. Swipe right to move an item to Grocery, left to keep it for later. Good for deciding together without texting.

Strengths
  • Puts your logging requirement front and center
  • Feels personal, made for two
  • Nice-to-have actually gets reviewed
Trade-offs
  • The list itself is one tap away
  • Feed gets noisy without grouping
  • Weakest in the store
Recommendation

Layer them instead of picking one

The concepts aren't mutually exclusive. The strongest app uses B as the main screen and adds the others as layers. Use the per-person marker colors from A on every screen, so it's always clear who added what.

Main screen: Aisle Flow (B)Grocery list by store and aisle, big checkboxes, trip mode, running total.
Second tab: Together Feed (D)Grouped activity log with reactions. Nice-to-have swipe deck lives here.
Slide-up sheet: Price Board (C)Opened from B's total. Ships when the Walmart and Harris Teeter price APIs are confirmed.

Questions for the two of you

Answers to these will shape the design

  1. Logging: should every change be logged, with multi-item adds grouped into one entry? Or did you mean something narrower?
  2. Do you shop together, or split up across stores at the same time?
  3. In store, pickup, or delivery at each of the four stores?
  4. Store memberships: Walmart+, Target Circle, Prime, Harris Teeter VIC?
  5. Your zip code or preferred store locations, since prices vary by location.
  6. Is about $15–50/month for Target price data worth it, or are receipt prices fine?
  7. Which concept feels most like you two, and which details from the others would you keep?
  8. Are you OK with $99/yr for Apple's developer program, so she can install through TestFlight?
